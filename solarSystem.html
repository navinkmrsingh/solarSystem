How this plate is drawn

Every body is placed by solving Kepler's equation (Newton iteration) from J2000 orbital elements — real eccentricities and inclinations, so Mercury swings hard, Pluto cuts clean through Neptune's orbit, and Halley rockets in from a 162° retrograde tilt.

The camera is an orthographic projection: world vectors are dotted into screen right, up and depth basis vectors built from yaw and elevation. Depth sorts the draw order, splits Saturn's rings into the halves passing behind and in front of the globe, and decides which surface features have rotated around the limb.

Planets are shaded, not textured: banded latitudes, projected surface features, then a terminator gradient driven by the true sun→body vector in camera space — which is why Mercury shows a crescent and a full disc at different points of its circuit.

Caveats: distances are compressed to r0.5 (switchable to true linear), and body radii are exaggerated by roughly two orders of magnitude — at true scale, Earth would be a sub-pixel speck.
